The Tower That Cooked a Car

London, 2013. A new glass skyscraper with a curved face focuses the summer sun onto the street below. It melts a parked car, scorches a doormat and fries a reporter's egg. Then it turns out the architect had done it once before.

Facts behind every joke

  • 20 Fenchurch Street, City of London, about 160 m tall, wider at the top. Its concave south face focused sunlight onto Eastcheap in late summer 2013. Press names: “Walkie-Scorchie”, “Fryscraper”.
  • A parked luxury car had its wing mirror, badge and panels melted. The developers paid the £946 repair bill.
  • A shop's doormat was scorched and bicycle parts melted. The City suspended three parking bays.
  • A City A.M. reporter fried an egg in a pan on the pavement; Sky News filmed one too. Spot readings of 91 °C and 117 °C were reported.
  • A permanent sunshade of horizontal aluminium fins was fitted in 2014. The building won the Carbuncle Cup (worst new UK building) in 2015.
  • The same architect's Vdara hotel in Las Vegas (opened 2009) had a curved south face whose “death ray” singed guests' hair and melted plastic by the pool in 2010.

Sources: Wikipedia: 20 Fenchurch Street, NBC News, IBTimes, Imperial College, Wikipedia: Vdara, Architectural Record.

Sound plan

  • Score: an original cheeky pizzicato-and-bassoon two-step at 112 BPM. It thins out to a held note for the explanation, then returns brighter for Las Vegas. Standard mix: music +3 dB, SFX −6 dB, voice unchanged, −14 LUFS.
  • Hook: a rising sizzle, then a drooping wobble as the car sags, then a white flash with a cymbal swell.
  • Street: key-fob chirp, footsteps, a melting “bloop” for the mirror, a rubbery warble for the wavy panels.
  • Heat: fat sizzle and an egg crack, a smoke puff for the doormat, a squish for the bike seat.
  • Weather: a soft rain hush that cuts dead when the sun comes out, then a “ta-da” glint.
  • Explanation: glassy pings for each bounced ray, a tight “zap” where they meet, and a pigeon flap.
  • Ending: ratchet clicks for the fins, a sad trombone for the award, and a pool splash on the last word.
