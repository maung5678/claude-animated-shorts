# "The Tower That Cooked a Car" — script v1 (2026-11-12)
Facts (sources: Wikipedia "20 Fenchurch Street" and "Vdara"; NBC News; IBTimes; Imperial College news; Architectural Record):
20 Fenchurch Street ("Walkie-Talkie"), City of London, ~160 m, wider at the top; concave south face focused sunlight onto
Eastcheap in late summer 2013 ("Walkie-Scorchie", "Fryscraper"). A parked car's wing mirror, badge and panels melted;
developers paid the £946 repair. A shop doormat was scorched and bicycle parts melted; three parking bays were suspended.
A City A.M. reporter fried an egg in a pan on the pavement. Spot readings of 91 °C and 117 °C were reported.
Permanent sunshade of horizontal aluminium fins fitted 2014. Carbuncle Cup (worst new UK building) 2015.
Same architect's Vdara hotel, Las Vegas: curved south face "death ray" singed hair and melted plastic by the pool, 2010.

In twenty thirteen, a brand new London skyscraper melted a car parked across the street.
It's late summer, and London's newest tower is a giant of shiny glass that gets wider as it goes up.
Its whole south side curves inward, like a huge glass bowl tipped toward the street.
One sunny afternoon, a man parks his shiny black car right underneath it.
He comes back a couple of hours later, and his wing mirror is drooping like a candle.
The badge has melted, and the panels have gone all wavy.
Down the street, a shop's doormat is scorched, and bike seats are going soft.
So a reporter takes a frying pan outside, cracks an egg on the pavement, and it sizzles.
Spot readings on that street hit a hundred and seventeen degrees.
Luckily, London is famous for cloudy weather.
Unluckily, that week, the sun showed up.
You see, a flat window bounces sunlight back and spreads it out.
But a window curved inward bounces it all toward the same small spot.
Ever burned a leaf with a magnifying glass? Same idea, except this magnifying glass is a hundred and sixty metres tall.
The owners paid for the car repairs, then bolted a giant sunshade of metal fins across the glass.
Two years later, it was voted the worst new building in Britain.
And the architect? His curved hotel in Las Vegas had been singeing sunbathers' hair by the pool, three years earlier.
