# "Snow Queen of Bethel" — original script v1 (2026-11-08)
Format: cartoon-3d-short (~60-68 s, ~3.7 words/s), one steady voice, our own characters + story.
Facts (sources below):
- Feb 1999: Bethel, Maine built "Angus, King of the Mountain", 113 ft 7 in, Guinness record (named for Gov. Angus King).
- Feb 2008: Bethel built a snowwoman over about a month: 122 ft 1 in (37.21 m), ~13 million lb of snow; Guinness record.
- A crane lifted buckets of snow to the top; volunteers stomped each layer into place ("layer cake" tiers).
- Eyes: wreaths (~5 ft). Eyelashes: 16 skis. Lips: car tires painted red. Arms: spruce trees (~25-30 ft). Buttons: skidder tires.
- Named Olympia, for U.S. Senator Olympia Snowe of Maine.
- She finally melted on 30 July 2008.
- 2020: Donnersbachwald, Austria built "Riesi" (~124 ft); Guinness did not verify it; Bethel's record stands.
- Physics: near 0 °C ice grains carry a thin liquid-like layer; pressed together they bond and refreeze (sintering), so
  "packing snow" holds a shape; very cold snow is dry powder that won't stick. Analogy: damp vs dry sand.

In 2008, a tiny town in Maine built a snowwoman taller than a ten-story building.
Bethel already held the world record, with a hundred and thirteen foot snowman from 1999.
But the town wanted more.
For a whole month, a crane hoisted bucket after bucket of snow to the top.
Volunteers stomped every layer flat, like the world's coldest layer cake.
Her eyes were giant wreaths. Her eyelashes were sixteen old skis.
Her lips were car tires, painted red. And her arms were two whole spruce trees.
When they were done, she stood a hundred and twenty-two feet tall, and weighed about thirteen million pounds.
They named her Olympia, after Maine's senator, whose last name, luckily, was Snowe.
But why does snow stick together at all?
Near freezing, every snowflake has a thin wet skin.
Press two flakes together, and that water glues them, then freezes solid.
It's like a sandcastle. Dry sand falls apart. Damp sand holds.
On a really cold day, snow is just powder, and you can't build anything.
That's why they stomped every layer: the pressure made the glue.
Olympia didn't finish melting until the end of July.
A town in Austria tried to beat her in 2020, but the record was never confirmed.
So the tallest snowperson in history is still a lady from Maine with skis for eyelashes.
